# Reward farming beats rebate scalping, Spain permitting

Yes, there are better uses of $10–50K on Polymarket than maker-rebate market making in 5/15-minute crypto Up/Down markets. Of the options checked, the strongest on evidence is **liquidity-reward farming in slow, long-dated markets**, with Polymarket's 3.25% holding reward stacked on top. It is paid per day for resting quotes whether or not they fill, and we estimate **roughly 10–30% a year net**. The second is an **information-reaction bot** that buys the side that has already won, at 90–99¢, in mentions, tweet-count, finance, economics and tech markets. In executed-trade data that earned **+1% to +5% per trade after fees**, but each market only takes a few hundred to a few thousand dollars. A third, overlapping route is passive maker bids that sell longshots, at about **+2.5% to +6% per dollar at risk**, with heavy tail risk. The baseline is weak: crypto Up/Down makers as a group net about **−0.09%**, and newcomers sit in the tiers losing **0.9–4.5% of their fills**. Pure arbitrage, copy-trading and cross-venue trades all fail after the 2026 taker fees. Builder businesses earn on users and order flow, not capital, so they are a different business. The ranking matters less than access. **Spain's gambling regulator ordered internet providers to block Polymarket and Kalshi nationwide on 26 May 2026**, and we found no final ruling as of 9 October. Polymarket US is open only to US persons, and evading a block with a VPN breaches Polymarket's terms. Every trading route below therefore needs Spanish legal clearance before it needs code. Unless another source is cited, the figures come from read-only pulls of Polymarket's public APIs on 9 October 2026 (Gamma, CLOB and Data API). These are short samples, so treat them as an order of magnitude, not a forecast.

> **Update, 10 October 2026: both open questions are now answered from public data** (full detail in [`pm_mm_check/REWARD_FARMING_VALIDATION.md`](../pm_mm_check/REWARD_FARMING_VALIDATION.md)).
> (1) **The leaderboard P&L excludes rewards and rebates.** I rebuilt 35 reward earners' all-time P&L from their activity history, and 27 of them matched trading-only P&L, small wallets within 0.2–2%. So the earner analysis below stands. All-time, 32 of 35 earners are net positive (median +$38.8K), but 20 lost money on trading, giving back a median 72% of their subsidies.
> (2) **A 30-day fill replay lowers the reward-farming estimate.** It simulated a new bot quoting in the 400 richest reward markets, scored on the second half of the window. In books with normal competition, **slow markets return about +5% to +14% APR**, and active markets return **+85% APR if fills are partial but −16% if informed traders sweep the full quote**. Quoting at the touch loses money in every case, and a worst-case sweep stress is negative everywhere. About 60% of the simulated reward comes from 8 near-empty books, where a newcomer would take most of the pool and earn several hundred dollars a day on $40K. But markets with scheduled data releases among them (UK CPI, Fed dissents, SpaceX market cap) lose money under the sweep stress.
> Read option 1 as **"about zero to modestly positive, and dependent on skill"**: find near-empty books, avoid scheduled releases, and pull quotes on moves. It is not a reliable 10–30%.

## Three routes beat the Up/Down baseline; five do not

The baseline sets a low bar but a real one. Across 5,328 resolved BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP Up/Down markets, the maker rebate was worth about **0.42% of filled notional**. Losses to better-informed takers cost about **0.50%**. Profits went to the top ~200 maker wallets, which earned +0.8% to +1.3% on their fills (about $170–1,400 a day each), while the other ~4,900 lost money ([Polymarket Data API](https://data-api.polymarket.com/trades), own analysis). A newcomer therefore starts in the losing tier. To beat this baseline, a strategy has to make money for an average entrant, not just for the best.

The ranking puts risk-adjusted evidence ahead of upside. On upside alone, the information-reaction bot (#2) could beat reward farming, because its capital comes back within hours instead of sitting in quotes. Nobody has measured how much of that flow a new bot can actually win. The ceilings of #1 and the baseline are also closer than the medians suggest. The top 40 reward earners had a median net of about **+$8.3K for the month** ([Data API leaderboard](https://data-api.polymarket.com/v1/leaderboard)), above a rank 51–200 Up/Down maker (about $5K a month) and well below a top-50 one (about $28K a month). The difference is the downside: most new reward farmers in slow markets lose a share of their rewards, while most new Up/Down makers lose money outright.

## Liquidity rewards pay for time in the book, and slow markets keep most of it

Polymarket's liquidity-reward program is a separate subsidy from maker rebates, and it is larger than the crypto rebate pool the baseline chases. The live reward list carries **$188,404 a day (about $69M a year) across 18,572 markets** ([CLOB rewards API](https://clob.polymarket.com/rewards/markets/current)). By comparison, the crypto rebate pool is roughly $14–17M a year ([Gamma API](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/markets/keyset), own analysis). The budget is uneven. The median market pays **$3 a day**, the top 300 hold 38% of the total, and 43 markets paying $500 a day or more hold $37K a day. Politics takes 45% and sports 20%. Crypto takes just 2.7%, and **no 5- or 15-minute Up/Down market carries rewards**: the temporary $1M August 2026 pool for those markets "has ended" ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards)).

The scoring rule decides where to quote. Each order scores ((v−s)/v)², where v is the market's maximum qualifying spread and s is the order's distance from the midpoint. The book is sampled once a minute at a random time, and each market's daily budget is split pro rata at midnight UTC, with a $1 minimum payout. One-sided quotes count at one third when the midpoint is between 10¢ and 90¢. Outside that range both sides are required ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards)). An order at half the maximum spread scores only a quarter as much as one at the midpoint. The rule therefore pays most for sitting exactly where fills are most likely to be informed, and that tension drives the economics.

We modeled a $1,000 two-sided quote against live books in 230 of the top-300 reward markets. Before any fills, it grosses a median **169% APR at the best bid/offer and 81% at half the max spread**. After a crude charge for adverse selection, the **median top-300 market turns negative** (−138% and −66%). That is because the richest budgets sit in news-driven markets: AI-release dates, ceasefire date ladders and MrBeast view counts, whose prices moved 30–68¢ in 30 days ([CLOB prices-history](https://clob.polymarket.com/prices-history), own analysis). Restricting to **"slow" markets**, whose 30-day price range is at most twice the max spread, changes the result. Those 63 markets carry $16.1K a day, and the modeled net is a median **+19% APR at half the max spread (86% of them positive) and +34% at the best bid/offer**. A $40K book spread over the 20 best slow markets models at about **$60 a day, or ~55% APR before fills**. The North Carolina data-center-moratorium markets pay about $2,000 a day each against 200–280K shares of competing quotes, so their yield holds up at the $20–50K scale. House-seat markets at $100 a day offer the best yield ratio but fill up after a few thousand dollars.

Wallet data shows how much of the gross survives. Among 171 wallets that trade the 60 largest-budget markets, the top earners collected **$42K–$132K of rewards in 30 days**. **27 of the top 40 lost money on trading itself**, giving back a median 47% of their rewards and rebates. Still, **31 of the 40 were net positive** ([Data API activity](https://data-api.polymarket.com/activity); [leaderboard](https://data-api.polymarket.com/v1/leaderboard)). The biggest farmer, Haradwaith, shows the trap: $132K of rewards against −$238K of trading P&L, after quoting fast markets on $11.9M of volume. Practitioners describe the working method as quoting away from the midpoint and cancelling "when the price moves towards your quote", and looking for "oddball/annoying markets with large rewards" ([EventWaves](https://eventwaves.substack.com/p/how-to-farm-liquidity-rewards-on)). The best-known open-source bot warns that market making "can lose money" ([poly-maker README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/warproxxx/poly-maker/main/README.md)). Gross yields of 30–60% in slow markets, minus the 30–70% the earner data says gets given back, give our **10–30% APR estimate**. On $25K that is roughly $7–20 a day. That range is a judgment, not a backtest. *(Update: the leaderboard is now confirmed to exclude rewards. The 30-day fill replay puts competitive slow markets at about +5% to +14% APR; see the note at the top.)*

Holding rewards add a modest layer. Polymarket pays **3.25% a year** (cut from 4.00%) on the midpoint value of positions in eligible events. The value is sampled hourly and paid daily from the treasury, and the program "is variable and subject to change" ([Polymarket Help](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364459-holding-rewards)). The live flags cover 339 markets with about **$130M of open interest**. They include the data-center-moratorium and bankruptcy ladders that also carry large liquidity budgets ([Gamma API](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/markets/keyset)), so inventory a farmer accumulates there earns both. The docs' own example counts Yes and No shares together. That suggests a matched pair earns about 3.25% on idle collateral with almost no price risk, which is **$325–1,625 a year on $10–50K**. That is a floor yield, not a strategy, and no source confirms that paired positions are paid without a cap. Both programs depend on Polymarket's discretion. Polymarket has also said it expects "a new reward and oracle-resolution system later this year" ([Polymarket Help](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364459-holding-rewards)). Any APR in this section is a snapshot of a subsidy, not a property of the market.

## Information beats bias: buying the side that has already won pays, broad mispricing bets do not

Polymarket in mid-2026 is close to calibrated, which rules out most "harvest the bias" strategies. Pooled non-sports markets from June to October 2026 have recalibration slopes of **0.92–1.10** depending on time to resolution. Bought at the midpoint, the 90–98¢ favourite band **lost 1.5% at 24 hours out** and gained 1.7% only in the final hour ([CLOB prices-history](https://clob.polymarket.com/prices-history), own backtest of 27,606 markets). A 1.2-billion-trade study of Polymarket's history finds a favourite–longshot bias of **under 1¢ per share before fees** ([Qin & Yang, arXiv 2606.04217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04217)). A Bocconi study found that mean-reversion, wallet-copying and directional strategies "fail after transaction costs" ([BSIC](https://bsic.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Polymarket-Microstructure-Analysis.pdf)). Weather is a good example. A calibrated model built on the NWS National Blend of Models had a coefficient of **−0.03 [−0.14, +0.09]** once the market price was included, meaning the forecast adds nothing beyond the price. Model-versus-market trading rules lost up to 22% per dollar after spread and fees ([Iowa Mesonet MOS](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/api/1/mos.json) and [Gamma events](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events), own backtest). Sports favourites at 90–98¢ also lose: −6.0% on spreads at 24 hours as a taker. In-play sports makers lose **9.4%** to faster takers ([Data API trades](https://data-api.polymarket.com/trades), own analysis).

The edge is in categories where the outcome is settled before the market closes. Prices there lag reality because whoever is resting orders reprices slowly. Executed trades show **mentions takers earning +4.4% [+1.9, +7.8] after fees** while makers lost 7.2%. Takers buying mentions favourites at 90–95¢ made **+7.5%**. In tweet and Truth Social count markets, favourite takers made +4.7% at 90–95¢ and +3.0% at 95–98¢. Over price history, buying 95–98¢ favourites one hour before the end earned **+3.1% in finance (241 bets), +3.2% in mentions, +3.4% in economics and +2.8% in tweet counts**. The pooled 95–98¢ bucket won 99.0% of the time against a break-even of about 96.9% ([Data API trades](https://data-api.polymarket.com/trades), [CLOB prices-history](https://clob.polymarket.com/prices-history), own analysis). In 5-minute crypto, takers buying at 95–98¢ in the final minute made **+3.25% [+2.53, +3.64]**. That is the same short-horizon information that beats Up/Down makers, and it is a latency race against bots running fast spot-price feeds. The repeatable version for a $10–50K bot is to watch live transcripts, the XTracker post count and exchange closes, then buy the decided side the moment it is decided. Fees are small at those prices: 0.04 × 0.95 × 0.05 ≈ 0.19¢ per share. The **taker rebate program** also returns 8–18% of fees at the volume tiers such a bot would reach ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/taker-rebates.md)).

The mirror trade, longshot selling, is the most consistent tilt in the executed data. Makers on the other side of 2–5¢ longshot buys earned **+2.5% [+1.1, +3.2]** pooled, +6.1% in tweet-count markets and up to +6.4% in 5-minute crypto. It fits the same quoting engine as reward farming, avoids taker fees and earns rebates. The economics are lopsided, though: you stake 90–98¢ to win 2–10¢. The confidence intervals also look tighter than the risk really is. Zero losses in 57 trades is still consistent with a true loss rate near 5%, above the ~3.5% break-even for a 96.5¢ favourite. Capacity is the other limit. Mentions markets trade only about **80,000 shares a day in total**, with a median of about 630 per market. Tweet counts trade about 1.6M shares a day.

Resolution risk is the tail for both trades. Disputes are rare, about **0.05%** of resolved markets in our sample ([Gamma API](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/markets/keyset)), although secondary reports count over 1,150 arbitrations in 2026, which suggests different definitions. When disputes do happen, voters follow the literal market text. Polymarket's "Strategy sells bitcoin by May 31" market resolved NO even though a filing showed 32 BTC sold in late May, because the confirmation came outside the market's window ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/markets/usd85m-polymarket-dispute-over-strategy-s-may-bitcoin-sale-puts-uma-s-token-voting-oracle-on)). About nine whale addresses reportedly hold half of UMA's voting power ([KuCoin/BlockBeats](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-dispute-resolutions-dominated-by-9-whale-addresses-critics-raise-concerns?lang=en_US)). A bot buying the decided side should skip markets with vague rules (e.g. "permanent deal", "closes airspace") and cap exposure per market. A strategy earning 1–5% per trade cannot absorb many −100% losses.

## The 2026 fee wall killed arbitrage and copy-trading

Taker fees reached crypto in January 2026, sports in February and most other categories on 30 March ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout)). This closed riskless arbitrage. A basket of outcomes now costs rate × (1 − Σp²) in fees, **about 2.5–4.5¢ per $1 basket**, and live books sit wider than that. Binary Yes+No arbitrage cannot appear at all, because the order book mirrors the two sides: in 3,976 markets, no Yes ask plus No ask came to less than $1. Across 7,788 complete multi-outcome events, **about $1 of genuinely riskless profit** was available at full depth. The Guinea-Bissau election events looked like $33 of profit, but their rules make it risky, not free. Buying No on every outcome found nothing that survived fees, and price ladders yielded **$0.46–0.52** in total ([CLOB books](https://clob.polymarket.com/books) and [Gamma events](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events/keyset), four sweeps). The often-quoted **$39.6M** of arbitrage extracted in 2024–25 predates fees and grouped fills up to an hour apart ([Saguillo et al., arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)). A 2026 NBA study found only seven executable single-market episodes, with a median life of **3.6 seconds** and executable size around **15 shares** ([Cheng et al., arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00864)). Naive scanners also flag "augmented" events, such as the 2028 nominee markets where prices sum to 0.85. Those lists omit some outcomes, so buying every listed candidate is really a bet that an unlisted one won't win, not an arbitrage.

Cross-venue gaps last longer but rarely clear the fees. The research literature finds **2–4% median deviations** between venues, which persist because contracts resolve on different rules, not because traders disagree ([Gebele & Matthes, arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706v1)). In live Fed-decision markets, Kalshi and Polymarket were within 0–2¢ of each other, and the best pair netted **−1.2¢ after fees** ([Kalshi API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?event_ticker=KXFEDDECISION-26DEC)). Polymarket's daily BTC "above" ladders tracked Deribit-implied probabilities within a median **1.3¢**, against a 3¢ spread. Across 44 strikes, the total edge after fees was about $60 ([Deribit API](https://www.deribit.com/api/v2/public/get_book_summary_by_currency?currency=BTC&kind=option), own analysis). Deribit's 0.1 BTC minimum contract (about $8K) also makes hedging small positions impractical. What survives is a **probabilistic maker strategy, not arbitrage**: resting bids on every outcome of 3-outcome sports and 5-bucket macro events so that the filled legs sum below ~0.98. That is market making with extra constraints, and it carries the risk of only some legs filling.

Copy-trading fails on persistence and timing. Wallet P&L rank correlations between consecutive periods are only **0.09–0.26**, and about 40% of the prior month's top-10 wallets simply stop trading. We copied the top-50 wallets' taker buys, size-weighted and net of fees. The result was about **−3% at a 1-minute delay and −13% at 60 minutes** on average, and only one of four monthly tests was positive, at +11% ([Data API](https://data-api.polymarket.com), own simulation). Academic work explains why. Profitable accounts mostly make money with limit orders, and **the top 1% of winners capture 76.5% of profits** ([Akey et al., CEPR DP21615](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cpr:ceprdp:21615)). Only 12% of top earners count as skilled ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study)). A copier can only follow as a taker, paying the spread plus a 1.5–3.5% fee. Wallets that look like insiders trade once in markets that charge no fee, and copying them knowingly is legally toxic. The bot ecosystem also carries **wallet-draining malware**, including npm packages that ask for private keys ([SafeDep](https://safedep.io/malicious-polymarket-npm-crypto-wallet-drainer/); [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/malicious-polymarket-bot-hides-in-hijacked-dev-protocol-github-org-and-steals-wallet-keys)).

## Builders earn on flow, not capital, and the other venues are closed

The builder program is the only route where income does not depend on the operator's own capital. Builders can charge up to **1% on taker flow and 0.5% on maker flow** routed through their code, on top of Polymarket's fees ([builder fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/builders/fees.md)). Verified builders also compete for weekly USDC rewards and grants of unpublished size ([builder tiers](https://docs.polymarket.com/builders/tiers.md)). The leaderboard is top-heavy. The #25 builder did about $2M in October's first nine days, roughly $7M a month, or **≈$34K a month at 0.5%**. The #50 did about $1.1M a month, **≈$6K at 0.5%**. Copy bots earn their money this way, from routed volume rather than users' returns: PolyCop has routed **$368M for 13,016 users** all-time ([builder leaderboard](https://data-api.polymarket.com/v1/builders/leaderboard)). These figures assume every routed dollar pays the full fee. Many builders charge less, and Polymarket can revoke fee rights at its discretion. Referrals pay little: 10% of net fees, and only for each referee's first 30 days. A referee trading $100K earns the referrer about $230 ([referral terms](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/referral-program.md)). Selling data is weak because the APIs are free and Polymarket bought Dome, the leading cross-venue data API ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/390546/polymarket-buys-fresh-prediction-market-api-startup-dome-marking-second-official-acquisition)). A small operator could plausibly tag its own bot flow under a Verified builder code to compete for weekly rewards. The value of that is unknown.

The other venues do not help a Spanish resident. **Polymarket US** is a CFTC-regulated exchange that requires US-person identity verification with a Social Security number ([signup docs](https://docs.polymarket.us/learn/get-started/signup.md)). Its new 7 October fee schedule (0.0695 taker, 0.0125 maker rebate) and its liquidity and volume incentive programs are irrelevant to a non-US person ([PM US fees](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees.md)). **Kalshi** charges 0.07 × P × (1−P) to takers in every category ([River Markets](https://www.rivermarkets.com/insights/kalshi-fees.html)). It filed to end its Volume Incentive Program no earlier than **13 October 2026** ([CFTC filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09282630083.pdf)). Its Liquidity Incentive Program **excludes non-US users** ([Kalshi help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)), and the same Spanish order blocks it.

## Spain's May 2026 block overrides every trading route

Spain is not on Polymarket's own geoblock list ([Polymarket geoblock docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/geoblock)), but that is not the deciding fact. On **26 May 2026 the DGOJ**, Spain's gambling regulator under the consumer-affairs ministry, opened sanction proceedings against Polymarket and Kalshi. It treats prediction markets as gambling that needs a Spanish licence, and it ordered internet providers to block both sites for users in Spain within 7–10 days ([Ministry press note](https://www.dsca.gob.es/en/comunicacion/notas-prensa/consumo-abre-expediente-sancionador-plataformas-polymarket-kalshi-ordena); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/spain-blocks-polymarket-kalshi-gambling-laws); [Euronews ES](https://es.euronews.com/2026/05/26/consumo-bloquea-cautelarmente-a-polymarket-en-espana-por-incumplimento-de-la-normativa)). Reports expected the proceedings to take 3–4 months. **We found no final resolution, fine or court ruling as of 9 October 2026**, in English or Spanish sources. The reporting describes enforcement aimed at the platforms. None of it addresses whether individual Spanish users face liability, so that question is open, not answered.

Workarounds are not an option. Polymarket's trade attestation requires users to confirm they are not "using a VPN", and Polymarket reserves the right to put a wallet into close-only mode ([polymarket.com/tos](https://polymarket.com/tos)). Since June 2026 it has reportedly blocked IP addresses associated with VPNs ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/polymarket-reportedly-tightens-vpn-rules-amid-rising-regulatory-pressure-june-1-2026)). A Spain-based operator running any of routes 1–4, 6, 7 or 8 would be trading on a venue the Spanish regulator has ordered blocked. A short consultation with a Spanish gaming lawyer comes before any engineering. Building tools or a builder product does not require placing trades from Spain, which makes it the cleanest of the options here. Spanish rules on promoting gambling could still apply, and we did not research them, nor Spanish tax treatment of prediction-market income. Our analysis ran on a US server that Polymarket geoblocks, so all data pulls were read-only and no order was placed.

## Conclusion

On Polymarket in 2026, the profit pools a small operator can reach are mostly **subsidies and speed**, not mispricing. The 2026 taker fees removed structural arbitrage and turned the favourite–longshot bias into rounding error. What remains is paid either by Polymarket's subsidy programs (about $69M a year in liquidity rewards and up to $4M in holding rewards, on top of the rebate pool) or by slow resting quotes that get picked off after an outcome is already known. That implies one piece of infrastructure rather than four separate strategies. A single quoting engine with cancel-on-move logic can farm rewards in slow markets, rest bids on near-certain sides in mentions, finance and tweet-count markets, and park inventory in holding-eligible events. A small event-reaction layer can then take the decided side when a word is said or a count is crossed. The same engine is what crypto Up/Down market making needs, minus the sub-second spot-price race. For a newcomer it aims at pools where the average participant comes out ahead.

Two caveats should shape the plan. First, the leading strategy's income is set by Polymarket's discretion. Its crypto reward pool already ended, the holding rate fell from 4.00% to 3.25%, and a new reward system is announced. Size commitments to survive budget cuts, and re-pull the reward list before every deployment. Second, every estimate here comes from days-to-months samples, and the 10–30% reward-farming figure is a judgment, not a backtest. Paper-trade the engine against recorded books for several weeks. Treat the Spanish legal question as the first gate, not the last.
